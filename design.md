# Airbase product design

## Product position

Airbase is the decision layer for choosing and working with AI models and tools. It should feel useful before sign-in: discovery and comparison are public, while one Airbase identity is the intended account layer for connected usage. The current repository is a preview and must distinguish sample catalog data from live, verified information.

## Visual direction

Use a quiet editorial surface with a deep ink hero and a clear, high-contrast catalog. The signature image is a responsive field of miniature AI-product marks: each tile is a distinct app-style monogram, and pointer proximity lifts, scales, and softly illuminates neighboring tiles in a damped wave. The effect should feel tactile rather than noisy, work without a third-party animation dependency, and become still for reduced-motion users. On touch screens it remains a static, legible logo field.

The palette pairs near-black navy and cool white with one electric blue action color and a restrained mint accent. Cards are rounded but not pill-shaped; thin cool-gray borders and whitespace do the work instead of heavy shadows. Use Manrope for display text and Inter for UI copy. Keep search, category, provider, price, speed, and context controls direct and readable.

## Interaction principles

- Search and category/provider facets should update the visible catalog immediately.
- Model cards should keep navigation separate from save and compare controls.
- Comparison is limited to three candidates and should show sample/provenance warnings alongside its metrics.
- Saved models and tools in this preview are browser-local; never describe them as account-synced.
- One Airbase account is the product direction. Do not route users to provider-specific sign-up as a substitute for a unified Airbase gateway.
- Do not present a simulated scan, benchmark, provider response, or price as a live result.

## Voice

Direct, curious, and precise. Prefer “Find the right model for this task” to broad claims about the “best” AI. Explain why a recommendation fits and show the trade-offs. Use visible freshness/provenance labels whenever performance or cost can change.
